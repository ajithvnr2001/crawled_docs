Cloudflare’s [_network_](https://www.cloudflare.com/network/) spans more than 330 cities in over 125 countries, where we interconnect with over 13,000 network providers in order to provide a broad range of services to millions of customers. The breadth of both our network and our customer base provides us with a unique perspective on Internet resilience, enabling us to observe the impact of Internet disruptions at both a local and national level, as well as at a network level.

As we have noted in the past, this post is intended as a summary overview of observed and confirmed disruptions, and is not an exhaustive or complete list of issues that have occurred during the quarter. A larger list of detected traffic anomalies is available in the [_Cloudflare Radar Outage Center_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/outage-center#traffic-anomalies). Note that both bytes-based and request-based traffic graphs are used within the post to illustrate the impact of the observed disruptions — the choice of metric was generally made based on which better illustrated the impact of the disruption.

In the first quarter of 2025, we observed a significant number of Internet disruptions due to _cable damage_ and _power outages_. _Severe storms_ caused outages in Ireland and Réunion, and an _earthquake_ caused ongoing connectivity issues in Myanmar. Russian networks were taken offline by a reported _cyberattack_ and purported _technical problems_, while a _fire_ took a telecom provider in Haiti offline briefly. In Q4 2024, we observed only a [_single government-directed Internet shutdown_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/q4-2024-internet-disruption-summary/#government-directed), and this quarter, **no such shutdowns were observed**. Unfortunately, this is an unusual occurrence, and in the three-year history of this blog post series, has only occurred previously in [_Q4 2023_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/q4-2023-internet-disruption-summary/#governmentdirected) and [_Q1 2022_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/q1-2022-internet-disruption-summary/). 

## Submarine and terrestrial cable damage

### Pakistan

Just after the new year, Internet connectivity in [_Pakistan_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/pk) was disrupted by a fault in the [_AAE-1 submarine cable_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/submarine-cable/asia-africa-europe-1-aae-1). According to a January 2 [_alert_](https://www.facebook.com/PTAOfficialPK/posts/pfbid02M2zM4MyFVAKWcHeg7WDKKTdwxF7PahWsYRW2W7WPLywp4dpLgS8m8ACUp5wtJJp6l) published on social media by the Pakistan Telecommunications Authority, the cable fault occurred near [_Qatar_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/qa), and would likely impact user experience across the country. Because there are seven submarine cables carrying international Internet traffic to/from Pakistan, the loss of AAE-1 did not cause an observable outage. However, the impact of the disruption was visible in the [_bandwidth_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/quality/pk?dateStart=2025-01-02&dateEnd=2025-01-04#bandwidth) and [_latency_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/quality/pk?dateStart=2025-01-02&dateEnd=2025-01-04#latency) graphs for Pakistan. On January 2 and 3, median latency peaked at around 125 ms, up from a pre-disruption median of approximately 80 ms. Concurrent drops in bandwidth were observed, with media download speeds dropping to around 6 Mbps from a pre-disruption media of around 9 Mbps. In an [_“Important Update”_](https://www.instagram.com/p/DEVff2gTxw9/) posted to their Instagram account, [_Pakistan Telecom (PTCL, AS17557)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/AS17557) also highlighted the potential for “slow browsing” — the [_Internet Quality graphs for that network_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/quality/as17557?dateStart=2025-01-02&dateEnd=2025-01-04) show similarly-timed shifts in median bandwidth and latency.

[_Pakistan_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/country/pakistan) is currently connected to seven submarine cables, with two additional connections on the way in 2026. This connection diversity means that damage to or an issue with one cable will likely have minimal impact on Internet availability within the country, as traffic can be re-routed across other paths.

### Syria

According to an [_announcement_](https://www.moct.gov.sy/news-0251) from the Syrian Ministry of Communications, a widespread Internet outage spanning January 23-24 was caused by sabotage that damaged two fiber optic cables that run along the highway between Damascus and Homs. The graphs below show that both [_HTTP_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/sy?dateStart=2025-01-23&dateEnd=2025-01-24#http-traffic) and [_DNS_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/dns/sy?dateStart=2025-01-23&dateEnd=2025-01-24#dns-query-volume) request traffic from [_Syria_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/sy) dropped to near zero between 00:30 and 03:30 local time on January 24 (21:30 on January 23 - 00:30 on January 24 UTC). Traffic began recovering shortly thereafter, and returned to expected levels by 09:00 local time (06:00 UTC). [_Announced IPv4 address space for the country_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/sy?dateStart=2025-01-23&dateEnd=2025-01-24), almost exclusively from [_Syria Telecom (AS29256)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as29256?dateStart=2025-01-23&dateEnd=2025-01-24), also saw an approximately 90% drop during this period, which suggests that these fiber cuts caused a significant amount of Syria Telecom’s network to become unreachable during the incident.

Echoing the disruption above, [_Syria_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/sy) experienced another Internet outage on March 25, again caused by sabotage that damaged fiber optic cables. According to an [_announcement_](https://moct.gov.sy/news-0279) from the Syrian Ministry of Communications, the damage occurred in the Maaloula and Hasiya regions, resulting in a near complete outage between 03:00 - 13:15 local time (00:00 - 10:15 UTC). Similar to the January outage, the graphs below show a near complete loss of [_HTTP_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/sy?dateStart=2025-03-24&dateEnd=2025-03-25#http-traffic) request traffic and a significant loss of [_announced IPv4 address space_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/sy?dateStart=2025-03-24&dateEnd=2025-03-25#announced-ip-address-space).

Somewhat paradoxically, [_DNS request volume from Syria_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/dns/sy?dateStart=2025-03-24&dateEnd=2025-03-25#dns-query-volume) was elevated during this outage, in contrast to the behavior observed during the January event. It isn’t clear what drove the additional traffic to Cloudflare’s [_1.1.1.1 DNS resolver_](https://one.one.one.one/dns/) in this case.

### Nepal

In early February, several Internet providers in [_Nepal_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/np) saw services disrupted when Indian provider [_Bharti Airtel (AS9498)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as9498) went offline. [_AS23752 (Nepal Telecom)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as23752), [_AS17501 (Worldlink Communications)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as17501), [_AS139922 (Dishhome Fibernet)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as139922), [_AS45650 (Vianet Communications)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as45650), and [_AS38565 (Ncell)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as38565), who all have Airtel as an upstream provider or peer, saw traffic disrupted between 21:00 - 22:30 local time (15:15 - 16:45 UTC) on February 2.

Published reports disagree on the underlying cause of the Airtel issue, with one source claiming that it was related to an [_ongoing payment dispute_](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nationwide-internet-outage-in-nepal-after-payment-dispute-with-indian-bandwidth-provider.html), while another claims that it was due to reported [_fiber cuts_](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/02/04/nationwide-internet-outage-raises-concerns-over-outstanding-dues) in Airtel’s network.

## Widespread power outages

### Angola

[ _Eleven provinces_](https://angop.ao/en/noticias/economia/restabelecida-energia-electrica-nas-11-provincias-afectadas-pelo-apagao/) in [_Angola_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ao) lost electrical power on January 6 [_due to_](https://x.com/portalangop/status/1876328913070432710) an interruption in the North and Center Interconnected System, according to the [_National Electricity Transmission Network (RNT)_](http://www.rnt.co.ao/). The widespread power outage disrupted Internet connectivity across the country, leading to a drop in traffic between 14:45 - 22:00 local time (13:45 - 21:00 UTC). [_Published reports_](https://angop.ao/en/noticias/economia/restabelecida-energia-electrica-nas-11-provincias-afectadas-pelo-apagao/) said that RNT was investigating the cause of the power outage, but no subsequent information was available confirming a specific cause.

### Sri Lanka

Monkey business at the Pandura electrical substation caused an island-wide power outage in [_Sri Lanka_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/lk) on February 9. More seriously, [_a monkey coming into contact with a grid transformer_](https://www.barrons.com/news/sri-lankan-monkey-causes-nationwide-blackout-2c3cc8ac) caused the power outage, which resulted in a multi-hour disruption to Internet traffic from the country. Traffic initially dropped around 11:30 local time (06:00 UTC), and recovered by around 21:00 local time (15:30 UTC). The graph below for [_AS18001 (Dialog)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as18001), a major Sri Lankan network services provider, illustrates the impact on traffic.

### Chile

On February 25, a [_massive power outage_](https://www.clarin.com/mundo/masivo-corte-luz-chile-afecta-80-ciento-pais_0_JxZR7Py3kb.html) in [_Chile_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cl) [_reportedly impacted_](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/chile-a-oscuras-mas-de-19-millones-de-clientes-sin-electricidad-equivalente-al-985-del-pais_20250225/) 98.5% of the country. A [_published report_](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/autoridades-informan-corte-luz-nacional-debe-desconexion-sistema-transmision-norte-chico_20250225/) noted that there was an interruption in the power supply from Arica to the Los Lagos region, caused by a disconnection of the 500 kV transmission system in the Norte Chico. The power outage resulted in an immediate and significant drop in Internet traffic, as seen at a country level, as well at a network level, as shown in the graphs below. Traffic initially fell at around 14:15 local time (18:15 UTC) and recovered to expected levels approximately 12 hours later, around 02:00 local time (06:00 UTC). It was [_reported_](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/gobierno-informa-mas-94-clientes-pais-ya-tienen-suministro-electrico_20250226/) that as of an hour after traffic had recovered, approximately 94% of customers had power restored.

### Honduras

A ground fault at the 15 de Septiembre electrical substation in [_El Salvador_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/sv) was [_reportedly_](https://www.elheraldo.hn/honduras/descartan-honduras-origen-apagon-afecto-region-erick-tejada-KD24633485) the cause of a power outage that resulted in a multi-hour Internet disruption in [_Honduras_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/hn) on March 1. The Regional Operator Entity (OER) [_stated_](https://www.instagram.com/protocinetico/p/DGrLkEqzWEP/) that the failure occurred at 09:22 local time (15:22 UTC), which resulted in traffic from the country dropping by about half. The disruption to Internet connectivity was relatively short-lived, as traffic returned to expected levels approximately two hours later.

### Cuba

According to an [_X post from @EnergiasMinasCub_](https://x.com/EnergiaMinasCub/status/1900714003623506167) (the Cuban state agency responsible for promoting the sustainable development of the country's energy, geological, and mining sectors), at around 20:15 local time on March 14 (00:15 UTC on March 15) “ _a failure at the Diezmero substation caused a significant loss of generation in the west of #Cuba and with it the failure of the National Electric System, SEN_ ”. This widespread power outage resulted in an immediate drop in request traffic from [_Cuba_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cu). Over the following two days, X posts from @EnergiasMinasCub, [_@OSDE_UNE_](https://x.com/OSDE_UNE) (the Cuban Electric Union), and [_@ETECSA_Cuba_](https://x.com/ETECSA_Cuba) (the Cuban Telecommunications Company) kept impacted subscribers apprised of the status of ongoing repairs. Traffic levels returned to expected levels around 20:00 local time on March 16 (00:00 on March 17 UTC), two full days after the incident began.

### Panama

An explosion and fire at the La Chorrera Thermoelectric Power Plant in [_Panama_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/pa) caused a [_massive power outage_](https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/world/blackout-in-panama-after-massive-fire-at-power-plant-water-supply-hit-too/ar-AA1B0vOv) across the country, starting at 23:40 local time on March 15 (04:40 on March 16 UTC). As expected, traffic dropped immediately, as seen in the HTTP and DNS request graphs below. However, recovery was fairly swift, as the [_electric system saw 75% recovery_](https://x.com/PanamaAmerica/status/1901288375778246675) by 03:00 local time (08:00 UTC), with full restoration [_completed_](https://x.com/Etesatransmite/status/1901234515999174723) at 06:08 local time (11:08 UTC). Traffic volumes began to increase after power was restored.

## Severe weather

### Ireland

[ _Storm Éowyn_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_%C3%89owyn) [_wreaked havoc_](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/live/2025/jan/24/storm-eowyn-uk-weather-scotland-ireland-warning-winds-live-updates) on [_Ireland_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ie) in late January, knocking out [_power and water_](https://transformers-magazine.com/tm-news/180000-without-power-in-ireland/), causing property damage, and limiting air and train travel. The storm’s impacts also disrupted Internet connectivity, as [_we observed_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1882851611749626219) traffic from Connacht and Ulster fall by 75% as compared to the previous week at 06:30 local time (06:30 UTC) on January 24. As recovery from the storm progressed over the next several days, Internet traffic gradually recovered as well, with traffic in the two provinces reaching levels near those seen the prior week by mid-day on January 28.

### Réunion

[ _Cyclone Garance_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Garance) made landfall over the French territory of [_Réunion_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/re) at ~10:00 local time (06:00 UTC) on February 28. Damage from the storm's 100+ miles/hour (160+ km/hour) winds caused power outages and infrastructure damage, resulting in disruptions to Internet connectivity. The most significant impacts to traffic were observed in the hours after the storm made landfall, but it took [_several days before traffic returned to expected levels_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/explorer?dataSet=netflows&loc=re&dt=2025-02-27_2025-03-05&timeCompare=1), reaching that point around 08:00 local time (04:00 UTC) on March 4.

## Earthquake

### Myanmar

On March 28 at 12:50 local time (06:20 UTC), a [_magnitude 7.7 earthquake_](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us7000pn9s/executive) shook [_Myanmar_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/mm), resulting in [_power outages and fuel shortages_](https://reccessary.com/en/news/asean-environment/massive-earthquake-in-myanmar-triggers-power-outages-fuel-shortages). Almost immediately, [_traffic dropped by around 40%_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1905566630760902897) at a country level. [_Regionally_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1905603328144261396), traffic from Nay Pyi Taw dropped 97% as compared to the previous week, Mandalay fell 90%, Ayeyarwady lost 88%, Bago 50%, and Shan State was down 38%.

While recovery efforts stretch into April, regular traffic patterns and volumes bounced back within days, as seen in the HTTP and DNS request graphs below.

However, at a network level, recovery has been mixed. Both [_AS134840 (MCCL)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as134840) and [_AS136442 (Oceanwave)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as136442) saw significant drops in traffic after the earthquake occurred, and traffic remained disrupted on both networks through the end of the first quarter. Peak traffic on MCCL has increased slightly, but nearly two weeks on, remains significantly lower than pre-earthquake levels. Traffic on Oceanwave saw steady growth after the initial disruption, and as of this writing is approaching pre-earthquake peaks. (It is unclear what caused the significant spike in request traffic seen from Oceanwave on April 3-4.) In contrast to these two providers, traffic from [_AS163255 (Mytel)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as163255) saw a significantly smaller disruption, and a [_significantly faster recovery_](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-business/humanitarian-communications/18289-mytel-says-connectivity-mostly-restored-after-myanmar-quake-amid-shutdown-fears.html), as did traffic from [_AS135300 (Myanmar Broadband Telecom)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as135300?dateStart=2025-03-27&dateEnd=2025-04-09#traffic-trends).

## Cyberattack

### Russia

On January 7, [_Russian_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ru) Internet provider [_Nodex (AS29329)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as29329) said in [_a post on Russian social media platform VKontakte_](https://vk.com/wall-7622_825) (translated) “ _Dear Subscribers, our technical staff is still working on restoring the network. The process is painstaking and long. We express our deep gratitude to those who support us in this difficult moment! This is really important for us. Let me remind you that our network was attacked by Ukrainian hackers, which resulted in its complete failure. At the moment, its functioning is being restored. There will be communication. When, is still unknown._ ” The [_Ukrainian Cyber Alliance_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Cyber_Alliance), a community of pro-Ukraine cyber activists formed in 2016, [_claimed responsibility for the attack_](https://t.me/UCAgroup/38) in a Telegram post.

The “complete failure” of the Nodex network is visible in the traffic graph below, where Internet traffic from the network began to drop after 03:00 local time (00:00 UTC) on January 7, reaching zero around 05:30 local time (02:30 UTC). Traffic from the network remained essentially non-existent until around 14:00 local time (11:00 UTC) the next day, recovering fairly quickly after that. Announced IPv4 address space fell by two-thirds at the same time that traffic volume dropped to zero, but recovered at 21:20 local time (18:20 UTC).

## Fire damage

### Los Angeles, California

Between January 7-9, during the early days of the[ _2025 Southern California wildfires_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Southern_California_wildfires) — which affected the [_Palisades_](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2025/1/7/palisades-fire) and [_Eaton_](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2025/1/7/eaton-fire) areas in Los Angeles — there were clear Internet disruptions in at least 13 Los Angeles neighborhoods. According to Cloudflare’s data, traffic drops of over 50% compared to the previous week were especially noticeable in cities like Pacific Palisades, Altadena, Malibu, Temple City, and Monrovia, among others. In the weeks that followed, traffic remained significantly lower than before the fires, particularly in Pacific Palisades and Altadena, reflecting the devastation in those areas. However, traffic recovery occurred significantly sooner in Malibu, Temple City, and Monrovia, although peak traffic levels remain somewhat below pre-fire levels.

### Haiti

On January 15, an [_X post_](https://x.com/jpbrun30/status/1879554628469362857) from the Director General of [_Digicel Haiti (AS27653)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as27653) stated (translated) “ _Dear customers, last night at 8:30 pm we suffered damage to 2 of our international fiber optic cables caused by a fire in the metropolitan area. At 10:30 am a 3rd outage affected all international services, Internet and Moncash. Our teams are mobilized to resolve the problem as quickly as possible._ ” These fires ultimately caused two complete Internet outages for Digicel Haiti’s customers, as seen in the graphs below.

Both traffic and announced IP address space (IPv4 & IPv6) dropped to zero between 20:30 - 21:45 local time on January 14 (01:30 - 02:45 on January 15 UTC) and again between 10:15 - 11:00 local time on January 15 (15:15 - 16:00 UTC).

## Technical problems

### Russia

On January 14, multiple [_Russian_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ru) networks, including [_AS8359 (MTS)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as8359), [_AS12389 (Rostelecom)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as12389), [_AS16345 (Beeline)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as16345), [_AS31133 (MegaFon)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as31133), and [_AS203451 (K-telecom)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as203451) all experienced a brief disruption in connectivity. As the traffic graphs below show, Internet traffic from these networks [_dropped by around 80%_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1879196319874740312) between 14:00 - 14:30 UTC. According to a [_statement_](https://meduza.io/en/news/2025/01/14/internet-users-in-russia-report-widespread-service-outages) from [_Roskomnadzor_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskomnadzor), “ _A brief connectivity issue was identified. Network operations were promptly restored._ ” However, [_industry observers suggested_](https://x.com/Liveuamap/status/1879195091874746543) that the cause may have been due to an update to the so-called “Russian firewall” that failed and was quickly rolled back.

### Georgia

Subscribers to [_Magticom (AS16010)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as16010), one of the largest Internet providers in [_Georgia_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ge), experienced a complete outage on January 27. Request traffic and announced IP address space disappeared at 21:25 local time (17:25 UTC), recovering at 01:55 local time on January 28 (21:55 UTC). A (translated) [_Facebook post from Magticom_](https://www.facebook.com/permalink.php?story_fbid=pfbid03i7LBfz1Rn3YUJAUgzgv8oKK2xTPoozMAzeJnEWxbqPvYkMSUukwYnCGSqJDRcpgl&id=100064447729415) explained that the company’s Internet connectivity comes through “ _channels from Europe_ ” and that “ _damage was reported in Turkey, where heavy snowfall and avalanche risks have prevented the partner company’s technical teams from reaching the affected area_ ”. Further, it noted that on the backup channel, “ _suspicious damage was reported at three points on the Georgian side, in the territory of Adjara…_ ” Magticom’s published start and end times for the outage align with the loss and recovery of traffic and announced IP address space observed in Cloudflare data. 

### France

Subscribers of [_Bouygues Telecom (AS5410)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as5410) in [_France_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/fr) experienced a brief disruption to their Internet connectivity on March 11. According to a (translated) [_X post from the provider_](https://x.com/bouyguestelecom/status/1899352941942681834), “ _Following a technical incident between 5 a.m. and 7 a.m. you may have encountered difficulties using your services._ ” As seen in the request traffic graphs below, a drop in traffic is visible between 05:00 - 06:45 local time (04:00 - 05:45 UTC), aligning with the provider’s stated timeframe. Bouyges Telecom did not provide any subsequent details around the cause of the “technical incident”.

## Unknown cause

### Syria

Major Internet outages and disruptions in [_Syria_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/sy) are generally well documented, such as the cable cuts discussed above. However, on February 3, a multi-hour disruption was observed in the country, but no underlying cause was ever publicly disclosed. Starting approximately 14:00 local time (11:00 UTC), traffic from the country dropped by approximately 80%, along with a ~60% drop in announced IPv4 address space. Both traffic and announced IP address space returned to expected levels around 23:00 local time (20:00 UTC). The outage was confirmed in an [_X post from Syrian Television_](https://x.com/syr_television/status/1886403431583092826).

## Conclusion

While the single government-directed shutdown last quarter, and the lack of such shutdowns this quarter, is an encouraging trend, we expect that it will be short-lived if countries like Iraq and Syria once again take such measures to prevent cheating on nationwide exams. As always, we encourage governments to recognize the collateral damage of such actions, and suggest that they explore alternative solutions to this problem.

Readers of a certain age may remember the so-called "dot com boom" that took place in the early 2000's. The boom's "dot com" is what is known as a Top-Level Domain (TLD). [_Originally_](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc920.html) intended to organize domain names into a small set of categorical groupings, over the past 40+ years, the set of TLDs has expanded to include country code top-level domains (ccTLDs, like [`_.us_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/us), [`_.pt_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/pt), and [`_.cn_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/cn)), as well as additional generic top-level domains (gTLDs) beyond the initial seven, such as [`_.biz_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/biz), [`_.shop_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/shop), and [`_.nyc_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/nyc). Internationalized TLDs, such as [`_.сайт_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/xn--80aswg), [`_.онлайн_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/xn--80asehdb),` [_.شبكة_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/xn--ngbc5azd)`, [`_.游戏_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/xn--unup4y), and brand TLDs, like [`_.google_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/google) and [`_.nike_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/nike) have also been added. As of October 2025, [_over 1,400 entries_](https://data.iana.org/TLD/tlds-alpha-by-domain.txt) can be found in ICANN's list of all valid top-level domains, and a further expansion is [_expected to begin in April 2026_](https://newgtldprogram.icann.org/en/application-rounds/round2).

[_Cloudflare Radar_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/) has long published [_domain ranking_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/domains) information, providing insights into popular and trending domains. And in February 2025, we [_added_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/new-dns-section-on-cloudflare-radar/) a number of [_DNS-related insights to Radar_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/dns), based on analysis of traffic to our [_1.1.1.1_](https://one.one.one.one/) Public DNS Resolver.

Building on this, today we are launching a [_new TLD page_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds) on Radar that, based on aggregated data from multiple Cloudflare services, provides insights into TLD popularity, activity, and security, along with links directly into [_Cloudflare Registrar_](https://domains.cloudflare.com/) to enable users to register domain names in [_supported TLDs_](https://domains.cloudflare.com/tlds).

## Initial security-related insights

Before today, Radar already offered insights into TLDs, though these were distributed across a couple of different pages and datasets.

In March 2024, when we [_launched_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/email-security-insights-on-cloudflare-radar/) the [_Email Security page_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/security/email), we introduced the [_“Most abused TLDs”_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/security/email#most-observed-tlds) metric. This chart highlights TLDs associated with the largest shares of malicious and spam email. The analysis is based on the sending domain’s TLD, extracted from the `From:` header in email messages, with data sourced from [_Cloudflare’s cloud email security service_](https://www.cloudflare.com/zero-trust/products/email-security/).

## A new aggregate overview based on DNS Magnitude

Today, we’re excited to announce the new [_TLD page_](http://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds) on Radar. The landing page and the dedicated per-TLD pages provide TLD managers and site owners with a perspective on the relative popularity of TLDs they manage or may be considering domains in, as well as insights into TLD traffic volume and distribution.

Located under the DNS menu, the landing page introduces a ranking of top-level domains based on [_DNS Magnitude_](https://www.icann.org/en/system/files/files/dns-magnitude-05aug20-en.pdf) — a metric originally developed by [_nic.at_](https://www.nic.at/media/files/pdf/dns-magnitude-paper-20200601.pdf) to estimate a domain’s overall visibility on the Internet.

Instead of simply counting the total number of DNS queries, DNS Magnitude incorporates a sense of how many unique clients send queries to domains within the TLD. This approach gives a more accurate picture of a TLD’s reach, since a small number of sources can generate a large number of queries. Our ranking is based on queries observed at Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 resolver. We aggregate individual client IP addresses into subnets, referred to here as "networks".

The magnitude value ranges from 0 to 10, with higher values (closer to 10) indicating that the TLD is queried by a broader range of networks. This reflects greater global visibility and, in some cases, a higher likelihood of name collision across different systems. [_According to ICANN_](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/name-collision-2013-12-06-en), a name collision occurs when an attempt to resolve a name used in a private name space (such as under a non-delegated Top-Level Domain) results in a query to the public [Domain Name System (DNS)](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/dns/what-is-dns/). When the administrative boundaries of private and public namespaces overlap, name resolution may yield unintended or harmful results. For example, if ICANN were to delegate `.home`, that could cause significant issues for hobbyists that use the (currently non-delegated) TLD within their local networks.

$Magnitude=\frac{ln(unique\ networks\ querying\ the\ TLD)}{ln(all\ unique\ networks)}*10$

The table displays a paginated ranking of the top 2,500 TLDs, along with several key attributes. Each entry includes the TLD itself — which links to a dedicated page for delegated TLDs — as well as its type:

  * [_gTLD_](http://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds?q=gTLD) (generic TLD): used for general purposes, such as [`_.com_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/com) or` [_.info_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/info)`.
  * [_grTLD_](http://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds?q=grTLD) (generic restricted TLD): limited to specific communities or uses, such as` [_.name_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/name)`.
  * [_ccTLD_](http://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds?q=ccTLD) (country code TLD): assigned to individual countries or territories, such as` [_.uk_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/uk)` or [`_.jp_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/jp).
  * [_iTLD_](http://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds?q=iTLD) (infrastructure TLD): reserved for technical infrastructure, such as [`_.arpa_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/arpa).
  * [_sTLD_](http://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds?q=sTLD) (sponsored TLD): operated by a sponsoring organization representing a defined community, such as [`_.edu_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/edu) or [`_.gov_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/gov).



The status column indicates whether the TLD is delegated, meaning it is officially assigned and active in the [_root zone_](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db) of the DNS, or non-delegated, meaning it is not currently part of the public DNS. The table also shows the manager of each TLD — typically the organization or registry responsible for its operation — and the corresponding DNS magnitude value.

While the top 10 TLDs include stalwarts such as [`_.com_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/com)/[`_.net_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/net)/[`_.org_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/org) and ccTLDs that have been commercially repurposed, such as [`_.io_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/io)/[`_.co_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/co)/[`_.tv_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/tv), the TLD at the top of the list may be a bit surprising: [`_.su_`](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.su).

This TLD was delegated for the Soviet Union back in 1990, but its use waned after the dissolution of the USSR, with constituent republics becoming independent and using their own dedicated ccTLDs. (ICANN reportedly [_plans to retire_](https://domainnamewire.com/2025/03/11/icann-moves-to-retire-soviet-era-su-country-domain-name/) `.su `in 2030.) Looking at a single day’s worth of data, the` .su` TLD does not rank #1 by unique networks. However, over a longer period of time, such as seven days, it sees queries from more unique networks than other TLDs, placing it atop the magnitude list. Further analysis of the top hostnames observed within this TLD suggests that they are mostly associated with a popular online world-building game. Interestingly, over half of the queries for .su domains [_come from_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/su#geographical-distribution) the United States, Germany, and Brazil.

## More detailed TLD insights

The new TLD section also offers [_dedicated pages_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/com) for individual TLDs. By clicking on a TLD in the DNS Magnitude table or searching for a TLD in the top search bar, users can access a page with detailed insights and information about that TLD. It’s important to note that while non-delegated TLDs are included in the DNS Magnitude ranking, TLD-specific pages are only available for delegated TLDs. The list of delegated TLDs, along with their type and manager, is sourced from the [_IANA’s Root Zone Database_](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db).

When a user enters an individual TLD page, they see two main cards. The first card provides general information about the TLD, including its type, manager, DNS magnitude value, DNSSEC support, and RDAP support. DNSSEC support is determined by checking whether the TLD has a [_Delegation Signer (DS) record_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/dns/dns-records/dnskey-ds-records/) in the [_root zone_](https://www.internic.net/domain/root.zone). We also parse the record to get the associated [_DNSSEC algorithm_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/dns/dnssec/how-dnssec-works/). [_RDAP_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/registrar/account-options/whois-redaction/#what-is-rdap) support is indicated if the TLD is listed in the [_IANA RDAP bootstrap file_](https://data.iana.org/rdap/dns.json). RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol) is a new standard for querying domain contact and nameserver information for all registered domains.

The second card contains [_WHOIS_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/dns/what-is-domain-privacy/) data for the TLD, including its creation date, the date of the last update, and the list of nameservers. If the TLD is supported by Cloudflare Registrar, an additional card appears, giving users direct access to registration options. As of today, Cloudflare Registrar supports [_over 400 TLDs_](https://domains.cloudflare.com/tlds).

Below these cards, the page features the [_DNS query volume_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/com#dns-query-volume) section, which presents insights based on queries to Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 resolver for domains under the TLD. This section includes a chart showing DNS queries over the selected time period, along with a donut chart breaking down queries by type, response code, and DNSSEC support. A choropleth map further illustrates the percentage of DNS queries by country, highlighting which regions generate the most queries for domains under the TLD.

Each individual TLD page also includes a [_Certificate Transparency_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/com#certificate-issuance-volume) section, offering visibility into [TLS/SSL certificate issuance](https://www.cloudflare.com/application-services/products/ssl/) for the TLD. When we [_launched_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/new-dns-section-on-cloudflare-radar/) the [_DNS page_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/dns) earlier in 2025, we provided query volumes by TLDs, but this was limited to ccTLDs. Today, we’re extending that dataset to include all delegated TLDs. With these new insights, we’ve added the [_“Top-level domain distribution”_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/dns#top-level-domain-distribution) section to the DNS page, featuring a line chart that shows the distribution of queries to 1.1.1.1 across the top 10 TLDs, alongside a table extending this ranking to the top 100. Not surprisingly, [_.com_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/com) tops the ranking with more than 60% of queries, followed by [`_.net_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/net), [`_.arpa_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/arpa) (an infrastructure TLD), and [`_.org_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/org).

It is also worth noting that both Radar search and the API support both [_punycode_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punycode) ([_A-Label/ASCII-Label_](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5890#section-2.3.2.1)) and [_internationalized domain name (IDN)_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalized_domain_name) ([_U-Label/UNICODE-Label_](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5890#section-2.3.2.1)) representations of non-ASCII TLDs. For example, the U-Label representation of the South Korean TLD [_.kr_](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/xn--3e0b707e.html) is written as 한국 and the A-Label representation is [`_xn--3e0b707e_`](https://radar.cloudflare.com/tlds/xn--3e0b707e).

## Looking ahead

Because TLDs are a foundational component of the Domain Name System, it is critical that the associated name servers are highly performant. Based on billions of daily queries to these name servers, we plan to add insights into their performance to Radar’s TLD pages in 2026. These insights will provide TLD managers with an external perspective on query responsiveness, and will give developers and site owners a perspective on the potential impact of the performance of the associated TLD name servers as they look to register new domain names.

The underlying data for these new TLD pages is available via the [_API_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/resources/radar/subresources/tlds/) and can be interactively explored in more detail using Radar’s [_Data Explorer and AI Assistant_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/explorer?dataSet=dns&groupBy=tld). And as always, Radar and Data Assistant charts and graphs are downloadable for sharing, and embeddable for use in your own blog posts, websites, or dashboards.

If you share our TLD charts and graphs on social media, be sure to tag us: [_@CloudflareRadar_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar) (X), [_noc.social/@cloudflareradar_](https://noc.social/@cloudflareradar) (Mastodon), and [_radar.cloudflare.com_](https://bsky.app/profile/radar.cloudflare.com) (Bluesky). If you have questions or comments, or suggestions for data that you’d like to see us add to Radar, you can reach out to us on social media, or contact us via [_email_](mailto:radar@cloudflare.com).

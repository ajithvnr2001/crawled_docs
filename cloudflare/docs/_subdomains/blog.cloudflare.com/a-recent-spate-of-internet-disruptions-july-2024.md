Cloudflare Radar is constantly monitoring the Internet for widespread disruptions. In mid-July, we published our [_Q2 2024 Internet Disruption Summary_](http://blog.cloudflare.com/q2-2024-internet-disruption-summary), and here we examine several recent noteworthy disruptions detected in the first month of Q3, including traffic anomalies observed in Bangladesh, Syria, Pakistan, and Venezuela.

### Bangladesh

[Violent student protests](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/south-asia/internet-shut-nationwide-bandh-announced-why-is-bangladesh-experiencing-deadly-protests/articleshow/111829956.cms) in Bangladesh against quotas in government jobs and rising unemployment rates led the government to order the nationwide shutdown of mobile Internet connectivity on July 18, [reportedly](https://therecord.media/bangladesh-mobile-internet-social-media-outages-student-protests) to “ensure the security of citizens.” This government-directed shutdown ultimately became a near-complete Internet outage for the country, as broadband networks were taken offline as well. At a country level, [Internet traffic in Bangladesh dropped to near zero](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/bd?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) just before 21:00 local time (15:00 UTC). [Announced IP address space from the country dropped to near zero](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/bd?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) at that time as well, meaning that nearly every network in the country was disconnected from the Internet.

The outage at [AS25245 (Banglalink)](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as45245) started at 02:15 local time on July 18 (20:15 UTC on July 17) as both [Internet traffic](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/as45245?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) and [announced IP address space](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as45245?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) dropped to zero.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![5](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW45STT2VKYYJA51KKCGJX77.png&w=715&h=402&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

At [AS24432 (Robi Axiata)](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as24432), an Internet outage was observed starting around 06:30 local time on July 18 (00:30 UTC), with both [Internet traffic](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/as24432?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) and [announced IP address space](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as24432?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) disappearing at that time.

[Announced IP address space](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as58715?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) began to fall at 21:00 local time (15:00 UTC), and was completely gone by 21:25 local time (15:25 UTC).

However, Cloudflare Radar’s [Internet Quality measurements](https://radar.cloudflare.com/quality/bd?dateStart=2024-07-14&dateEnd=2024-07-20) at a country level show a clear increase in median bandwidth and a concurrent drop in median latency, both likely due to the loss of measurements from mobile network providers as they disconnected from the Internet.

Five days after the full Internet shutdown started, broadband Internet services providers in Bangladesh began to restore connectivity on July 23. The initial restoration was characterized as a “trial run”, prioritizing banking, commercial sectors, technology firms, exporters, outsourcing providers and media outlets, [according to](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/352554/broadband-internet-restored-in-limited-areas-after) the state minister for post, telecommunication and information technology. [Announced IP address space](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/bd?dateStart=2024-07-23&dateEnd=2024-07-23) began to increase around 19:00 local time (13:00 UTC), with traffic volumes beginning to trend upwards at that same time, as selected networks reconnected to the Internet.

### Syria

Unfortunately, Syria is no stranger to Internet shutdowns, as they occur yearly during nationwide exams, implemented with the intent of preventing cheating on those exams. Our recent blog post titled [_Exam-ining recent Internet shutdowns in Syria, Iraq, and Algeria_](http://blog.cloudflare.com/syria-iraq-algeria-exam-internet-shutdown) examined the first round of 2024 exams, which took place between May 26 and June 13. A second round of exams, and with them, multi-hour Internet shutdowns, began on July 25, and seen in the schedules below, [published by Syrian Telecom on its Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/photo/?fbid=862569062570288&set=a.449047400589125) (English translation via Google Lens).

### Pakistan

Closing out the month, on July 31, Pakistan experienced a wide-scale Internet disruption that lasted approximately two hours, between 13:30 - 15:30 local time (08:30 - 10:30 UTC). Traffic only dropped ~45% at a country level, but [AS17557 (PTCL)](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as17557) experienced a near complete loss of traffic, while traffic at [AS24499 (Telenor Pakistan)](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as24499) dropped nearly 90%. Together, the two network providers serve an estimated nine million users, and are among the top five Internet service providers in the country.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![19](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW47ZQQQ9DX0B9VP9K9TQDJ6.png&w=715&h=402&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

It was [reported](https://www.globalvillagespace.com/internet-outage-in-pakistan/) that the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) attributed the disruptions to a technical glitch in the international submarine cable affecting the Pakistan Telecommunication Company Limited (PTCL) network. However, another [published report](https://incpak.com/national/internet-services-outtage-across-pakistan/) noted “ _According to our sources, the government’s latest firewall edition to block the content was misconfigured, resulting in Internet connectivity disruption._ ” (Some additional information about the firewall can be found in [this article](https://www.dawn.com/news/1847040).) The graphs below are from forthcoming TCP reset/timeout data on Cloudflare Radar, and show increased numbers of connections terminating immediately after the initial synchronization (SYN) packet used to establish new TCP connections (“[Post SYN](https://developers.cloudflare.com/radar/glossary/#tcp-resets-and-timeouts)”) between 13:30 - 15:30 local time (08:30 - 10:30 UTC) on PTCL and Telenor Pakistan, coincident with the observed disruption. In other words, the rate of SYN packets arriving at Cloudflare was mostly consistent during the disruption, but there was a drop in other TCP packets, suggesting that the firewall explanation may be plausible. A [Facebook post](https://www.facebook.com/PTAOfficialPK/posts/pfbid0VR44dAeAbo4ciChBVWzxUSkkxR3XGPmjppwJbV7UxsNStqiPMAtRpf7QtdcWq5tAl) from the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) simply highlighted that the issue had been resolved, and that “ _The exact issue is being investigated by PTA to avoid such instances in future._ ”

Analysts attributed the sudden drop to panic selling as investors struggled with limited market data."_

### Venezuela

In the past, some countries have implemented government-directed Internet shutdowns as a means of limiting communication about or organizing of protests and demonstrations associated with contested elections. Although such protests and demonstrations sprang up in the wake of a [contested presidential election in Venezuela](https://apnews.com/article/venezuela-presidential-election-maduro-machado-edmundo-results-acee6c8cd3a8fc88086c2dd71963b759) that took place on July 28, Internet shutdowns did not follow. However, in monitoring Internet traffic in Venezuela during the days around the election, the Cloudflare Radar team did observe several notable drops in traffic, as compared to the same times the week prior. After [surging 35%](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1817968310191038670) at 05:00 local time (09:00 UTC) on Sunday, July 28 (election day), traffic dropped after the polls opened, down by as much as 23% at 09:00 local time (13:00 UTC). [On July 29](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1818218146903236799), the day following the election, traffic was as much as 28% lower than the same time the previous week at 06:15 local time (10:15 UTC) and 18:45 local time (22:45 UTC).

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![24](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW4998745ZY7K13QJGZGQERJ.png&w=715&h=230&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

And while the observed drops in traffic appeared to be organic, and not caused by an Internet shutdown, it is worth noting that multiple websites are being blocked in Venezuela. An [Internet Society Pulse blog post](https://pulse.internetsociety.org/blog/internet-censorship-verging-on-service-blocking-ahead-of-venezuela-elections), published two days ahead of the election, reports that “ _Around 60 websites are currently blocked in Venezuela, including eight media sites and three that fact-check news and misinformation._ ”, citing data from the [Open Observatory of Network Interference (OONI)](https://explorer.ooni.org/chart/mat?test_name=web_connectivity&axis_x=measurement_start_day&since=2024-07-01&until=2024-07-26&time_grain=day&probe_cc=VE&axis_y=domain).

## Persistent Logs for every Worker

Starting today in open beta, you can automatically retain logs from your Worker, with full search, query, and filtering capabilities available directly within the Cloudflare dashboard. All newly created Workers will have this setting automatically enabled. This marks the first step in the development of our observability platform, following [_Cloudflare’s acquisition of Baselime_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-acquires-baselime-expands-observability-capabilities/).

Getting started is easy – just add two lines to your Worker’s wrangler.toml and redeploy:
    
    
    [observability]
    enabled = true
    

Workers Logs allow you to view all logs emitted from your Worker. When enabled, each `console.log` message, error, and exception is published as a separate event. Every Worker invocation (i.e. requests, alarms, rpc, etc.) also publishes an enriched execution log that contains invocation metadata. You can view logs in the `Logs` tab of your Worker in the dashboard, where you can filter on any event field, such as time, error code, message, or your own custom field.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 2](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW46AY245Z48FWN9ECW8VKZS.png&w=715&h=425&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

If you’ve ever had to piece together the puzzle of unusual metrics, such as a spike in errors or latency, you know how frustrating it is to connect metrics to traces and logs that often live in independent data silos. Workers Logs is the first piece of a new observability platform we are building that helps you easily correlate telemetry data, and surfaces insights to help you _understand_. We’ll structure your telemetry data so you have the full context to ask the right questions, and can quickly and easily analyze the behavior of your applications. This is just the beginning for observability tools for Workers. We are already working on automatically emitting distributed traces from Workers, with real time errors and wide, high dimensionality events coming soon as well.

## Connect to private databases from Workers

Starting today, you can now use [_Hyperdrive_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/), [_Cloudflare Tunnels_](https://www.cloudflare.com/en-ca/products/tunnel/) and [_Access_](https://www.cloudflare.com/zero-trust/products/access/) together to securely connect to databases that are isolated in a private network. 

[ _Hyperdrive_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/) enables you to build on Workers with your existing regional databases. It accelerates database queries using Cloudflare’s network, caching data close to end users and pooling connections close to the database. But there’s been a major blocker preventing you from building with Hyperdrive: network isolation.

The majority of databases today aren’t publicly accessible on the Internet. Data is highly sensitive and placing databases within private networks like a [_virtual private cloud (VPC)_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/cloud/what-is-a-virtual-private-cloud/) keeps data secure. But to date, that has also meant that your data is held captive within your cloud provider, preventing you from building on Workers. 

Today, we’re enabling Hyperdrive to [_securely connect to private databases_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/configuration/connect-to-private-database/) using [_Cloudflare Tunnels_](https://www.cloudflare.com/en-ca/products/tunnel/) and [_Cloudflare Access_](https://www.cloudflare.com/zero-trust/products/access/). With a Cloudflare Tunnel running in your private network, Hyperdrive can securely connect to your database and start speeding up your queries.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 4](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW46A3MEXRDZ9VXP1EXD95KX.png&w=715&h=386&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

With this update, Hyperdrive makes it possible for you to build full-stack applications on Workers with your existing databases, network-isolated or not. Whether you’re using [_Amazon RDS_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/examples/aws-rds-aurora/), [_Amazon Aurora_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/examples/aws-rds-aurora/), [_Google Cloud SQL_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/examples/google-cloud-sql/), [_Azure Database_](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-gb/products/category/databases), or any other provider, Hyperdrive can connect to your databases and optimize your database connections to provide the fast performance you’ve come to expect with building on Workers.

## Improved Node.js compatibility is now GA

Earlier this month, we [_overhauled our support for Node.js APIs in the Workers runtime_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/more-npm-packages-on-cloudflare-workers-combining-polyfills-and-native-code/). With [_twice as many Node APIs_](https://workers-nodejs-compat-matrix.pages.dev/) now supported on Workers, you can now use a wider set of NPM packages to build a broader range of applications. Today, we’re happy to announce that improved Node.js compatibility is GA.

To give it a try, enable the nodejs_compat compatibility flag, and set your compatibility date to on or after 2024-09-23:
    
    
    compatibility_flags = ["nodejs_compat"]
    compatibility_date = "2024-09-23"
    

Read the [_developer documentation_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/nodejs/) to learn more about how to opt-in your Workers to try it today. If you encounter any bugs or want to report feedback, [_open an issue_](https://github.com/cloudflare/workers-sdk/issues/new?assignees=&labels=bug&projects=&template=bug-template.yaml&title=%F0%9F%90%9B+BUG%3A).

## Build frontend applications on Workers with Static Asset Hosting

Starting today in open beta, you now can upload and serve HTML, CSS, and client-side JavaScript directly as part of your Worker. This means you can build dynamic, server-side rendered applications on Workers using popular frameworks such as Astro, Remix, Next.js and Svelte (full list [_here_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/frameworks)), with more coming soon.

You can now deploy applications to Workers that previously could only be deployed to Cloudflare Pages and use features that are not yet supported in Pages, including [_Logpush_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logging/logpush/), [_Hyperdrive_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/#_top), [_Cron Triggers_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/cron-triggers/), [_Queue Consumers_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/configure-queues/#consumer), and [_Gradual Deployments_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/versions-and-deployments/). 

To get started, create a new project with [_create-cloudflare_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/frameworks). For example, to create a new Astro project: 
    
    
    npm create cloudflare@latest -- my-astro-app --framework=astro --experimental
    

Visit our [_developer documentation_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/) to learn more about setting up a new front-end application on Workers and watch a [_quick demo_](https://youtu.be/W45MIi_t_go) to learn about how you can deploy an existing application to Workers. Static assets aren’t just for Workers written in JavaScript! You can serve static assets from [_Workers written in Python_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/languages/python/) or even [_deploy a Leptos app using workers-rs_](https://github.com/cloudflare/workers-rs/tree/main/templates/leptos/README.md).

If you’re wondering “ _What about Pages?”_ — rest assured, Pages will remain fully supported. We’ve heard from developers that as we’ve added new features to Workers and Pages, the choice of which product to use has become challenging. We’re closing this gap by bringing asset hosting, CI/CD and Preview URLs to Workers this Birthday Week.

To make the upfront choice Cloudflare Workers and Pages more transparent, we’ve created a [_compatibility matrix_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/compatibility-matrix/). Looking ahead, we plan to bridge the remaining gaps between Workers and Pages and provide ways to migrate your Pages projects to Workers.

## Cloudflare joins OpenNext to deploy Next.js apps to Workers

Starting today, as an early developer preview, you can use [_OpenNext_](https://opennext.js.org//cloudflare) to deploy Next.js apps to Cloudflare Workers via [_@opennextjs/cloudflare_](https://npmjs.org/@opennextjs/cloudflare), a new npm package that lets you use the [_Node.js “runtime” in Next.js_](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/building-your-application/rendering/edge-and-nodejs-runtimes) on Workers.

This new adapter is powered by our [_new Node.js compatibility layer_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/more-npm-packages-on-cloudflare-workers-combining-polyfills-and-native-code/), newly introduced _Static Assets for Workers_, and Workers KV, which is [_now up to 3x faster_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/faster-workers-kv). It unlocks support for [_Incremental Static Regeneration (ISR)_](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/building-your-application/data-fetching/incremental-static-regeneration), [_custom error pages_](https://nextjs.org/docs/pages/building-your-application/routing/custom-error), and other Next.js features that our previous adapter, [_@cloudflare/next-on-pages_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/framework-guides/nextjs/ssr/get-started/), could not support, as it was only compatible with the Edge “runtime” in Next.js.

[_Cloud providers shouldn’t lock you in_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/aws-egregious-egress/). Like cloud compute and storage, open source frameworks should be portable — you should be able to deploy them to different cloud providers. The goal of the OpenNext project is to make sure you can deploy Next.js apps to any cloud platform, originally to AWS, and now Cloudflare. We’re excited to contribute to the OpenNext community, and give developers the freedom to run on the cloud that fits their applications needs (and [_budget_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/workers-pricing-scale-to-zero/)) best.

To get started by reading the [_OpenNext docs_](https://opennext.js.org//cloudflare/get-started), which provide examples and a guide on how to add [_@opennextjs/cloudflare_](https://npmjs.org/@opennextjs/cloudflare) to your Next.js app.

We want your feedback! Report issues and contribute code at [_opennextjs/opennextjs-cloudflare on GitHub_](https://github.com/opennextjs/opennextjs-cloudflare/), and join the discussion on the [_OpenNext Discord_](https://discord.gg/WUNsBM69).
    
    
    npm create cloudflare@latest -- my-next-app --framework=next --experimental
    

## Continuous Integration & Delivery (CI/CD) with Workers Builds

Now in open beta, you can connect a GitHub or GitLab repository to a Worker, and Cloudflare will automatically build and deploy your changes each time you push a commit. Workers Builds provides an integrated [CI/CD workflow](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/serverless/glossary/what-is-ci-cd/) you can use to build and deploy everything from full-stack applications built with the most popular frameworks to simple static websites. Just add your build command and let Workers Builds take care of the rest.

## Safely release to production with Gradual Deployments

At Developer Week, we launched [_Gradual Deployments_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/versions-and-deployments/gradual-deployments/#_top) for Workers and Durable Objects to make it safer and easier to deploy changes to your applications. Gradual Deployments is now GA — we have been using it ourselves at Cloudflare for mission-critical services built on Workers since early 2024.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 7](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW4557PN8V9R24ZMZ4JX9RCE.png&w=715&h=357&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Gradual deployments can help you stay on top of availability SLAs and minimize application downtime by surfacing issues early. Internally at Cloudflare, every single service built on Workers uses gradual deployments to roll out new changes. Each new version gets released in stages —– 0.05%, 0.5%, 3%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% with time to soak between each stage. Throughout the roll-out, we keep an eye on metrics (which are often instrumented with [_Workers Analytics Engine_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/analytics/analytics-engine/)!) and we [_roll back_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/versions-and-deployments/rollbacks/) if we encounter issues. 

Using gradual deployments is as simple as swapping out the [_wrangler commands_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/wrangler/commands/#versions), [_API endpoints_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/operations/worker-versions-upload-version), and/or using “Save version” in the code editor that is built into the Workers dashboard. Read the [_developer documentation_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/versions-and-deployments/) to learn more and get started. 

## Queues is GA, with higher throughput and concurrency limits

[ _Cloudflare Queues_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/) is now generally available with higher limits. 

Queues let a developer decouple their Workers into event driven services. _Producer_ Workers write events to a Queue, and _consumer_ Workers are invoked to take actions on the events. For example, you can use a Queue to decouple an e-commerce website from a service which sends purchase confirmation emails to users.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 8](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW465EDQW6D7GRDY4WPAPVPA.png&w=715&h=256&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Throughput and concurrency limits for Queues are now significantly higher, which means you can push more messages through a Queue, and consume them faster.

  * **Throughput:** Each queue can now process 5000 messages per second (previously 400 per second).
  * **Concurrency:** Each queue can now have up to 250 [_concurrent consumers_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/consumer-concurrency/) (previously 20 concurrent consumers). 



Since we [_announced Queues in beta_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-cloudflare-queues/), we’ve added the following functionality:

  * [_Batch sizes can be customized_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/#batching), to reduce the number of consumer Worker invocations and thus reduce cost.
  * [_Individual messages can be delayed_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/#delay-messages), so you can back off due to external API rate limits.
  * [_HTTP Pull consumers_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/pull-consumers/) allow messages to be consumed outside Workers, with zero data egress costs.



Queues can be used by any developer on a Workers Paid plan. Head over to our [_getting started __ guide_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/get-started/) to start building with Queues.

## Event notifications for R2 is now GA

We’re excited to announce that event notifications for R2 is now generally available. Whether it’s kicking off image processing after a user uploads a file or triggering a sync to an external data warehouse when new analytics data is generated, many applications need to be able to reliably respond when events happen. [_Event notifications_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/r2-events-gcs-migration-infrequent-access/#event-notifications-open-beta) for [_Cloudflare R2_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/) give you the ability to build event-driven applications and workflows that react to changes in your data.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 9](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW48JXQVQW061B92FCWVRMCX.png&w=715&h=229&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Here’s how it works: When data in your R2 bucket changes, event notifications are sent to your queue. You can consume these notifications with a [_consumer Worker_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/reference/how-queues-works/#create-a-consumer-worker)or [_pull them over HTTP_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/pull-consumers/) from outside of Cloudflare Workers.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 10](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW46P6F4T0FXHZAM74HRSCXZ.png&w=715&h=561&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Since we introduced event notifications in [_open beta_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/r2-events-gcs-migration-infrequent-access/#event-notifications-open-beta) earlier this year, we’ve made significant improvements based on your feedback:

  * We increased reliability of event notifications with throughput improvements from Queues. R2 event notifications can now scale to thousands of writes per second.
  * You can now configure event notifications directly from the Cloudflare dashboard (in addition to [_Wrangler_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/wrangler/commands/#notification-create)).
  * There is now support for receiving notifications triggered by [_object lifecycle deletes_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/).
  * You can now set up multiple notification rules for a single queue on a bucket.



Visit [_our documentation_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/event-notifications/) to learn about how to set up event notifications for your R2 buckets.

## Removing the serverless microservices tax: No more request fees for Service Bindings and Tail Workers

Earlier this year, we quietly changed Workers pricing to lower your costs. As of July 2024, you are no longer charged for requests between Workers on your account made via [_Service Bindings_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/service-bindings/), or for invocations of [_Tail Workers._](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logging/tail-workers/) For example, let’s say you have the following chain of Workers: 

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![Diagram of Workers using Service Bindings and a Tail Worker](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW44H5BRDMPR9MEQB2BW7W5X.png&w=715&h=320&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Each request from a client results in three Workers invocations. Previously, we charged you for each of these invocations, plus the CPU time for each of these Workers. With this change, we only charge you for the first request from the client, plus the CPU time used by each Worker.

This eliminates the additional cost of breaking a monolithic serverless app into microservices. In 2023, we introduced new [_pricing based on CPU time_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/workers-pricing-scale-to-zero/), rather than duration, so you don’t have to worry about being billed for time spent waiting on I/O. This includes I/O to other Workers. With this change, you’re only billed for the first request in the chain, eliminating the other additional cost of using multiple Workers.

When you build microservices on Workers, you face fewer trade offs than on other compute platforms. Service bindings have [_zero network overhead_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/service-bindings/) by default, a built-in [_JavaScript RPC system_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/javascript-native-rpc/), and a security model with [_fewer footguns and simpler configuration_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/workers-environment-live-object-bindings/). We’re excited to improve this further with this pricing change.

## Image optimization is available to everyone for free — no subscription needed

Starting today, you can use [_Cloudflare Images_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/images/transform-images/transform-via-url/) for free to optimize your images with up to 5,000 transformations per month.

Large, oversized images can throttle your application speed and page load times. We built [_Cloudflare Images_](https://developers.cloudflare.com/images/) to let you dynamically optimize images in the correct dimensions and formats for each use case, all while storing only the original image.

In the spirit of Birthday Week, we’re making image optimization available to everyone with a Cloudflare account, no subscription needed. You’ll be able to use Images to transform images that are stored outside of Images, such as in R2.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-2517 12](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW46H9A57HY8RR745Y9F29YW.png&w=715&h=220&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Transformations are served from your zone through a specially formatted URL with parameters that specify how an image should be optimized. For example, the transformation URL below uses the `format` parameter to automatically serve the image in the most optimal format for the requesting browser:
    
    
    https://example.com/cdn-cgi/image/format=auto/thumbnail.png

This means that the original PNG image may be served as AVIF to one user and WebP to another. Without a subscription, transforming images from remote sources is free up to 5,000 unique transformations per month. Once you exceed this limit, any already cached transformations will continue to be served, but you’ll need a [_paid Images plan_](https://dash.cloudflare.com/?to=/:account/images) to request new transformations or to purchase storage within Images.

To get started, navigate to [_Images in the dashboard_](https://dash.cloudflare.com/?to=/:account/images) to enable transformations on your zone.

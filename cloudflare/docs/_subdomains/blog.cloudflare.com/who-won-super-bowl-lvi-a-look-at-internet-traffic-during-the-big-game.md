>  _“It's ridiculous for a country to get all worked up about a game—except the Super Bowl, of course. Now that's important.”_ \- [**Andy Rooney**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Rooney), American radio and television writer

When the Super Bowl is on, there are more winners than just one of the teams playing, especially when we look at Internet trends. By now, everyone knows that the [Los Angeles Rams](https://www.therams.com/) won, but we also want to look at which Super Bowl advertisers were the biggest winners, and how traffic to food delivery services, social media and messaging apps, and sports and betting websites changed throughout the game.

We covered some of these questions during our Super Bowl live-tweeting on our [Cloudflare Radar account](https://twitter.com/cloudflareradar). (Hint: follow us if you’re interested in Internet trends).

[Cloudflare Radar](https://radar.cloudflare.com/) uses a variety of sources to provide aggregate information about Internet traffic and attack trends. In this blog post, as we did [last year](https://blog.cloudflare.com/who-won-super-bowl-lv/), we use DNS name resolution data to estimate traffic to websites. We can’t see who visited the websites mentioned, or what anyone did on the websites, but DNS can give us an estimate of the interest generated by the ads or across a set of sites in the categories listed above.

The baseline value for the charts (that are only focused on US traffic) was calculated by taking the mean traffic level for the associated websites during 12:00 - 15:00 EST on Super Bowl Sunday (February 13, 2022).

### The Internet Impact of Commercials

Historically, many people have watched the Super Bowl as much for the ads as the actual football game. (Maybe even more so some years…) Many of the advertisements are now posted online ahead of Super Bowl Sunday. Given that, do these commercials still drive traffic to the company’s web site while the game is on?” As we saw in [2021](https://blog.cloudflare.com/who-won-super-bowl-lv/), the answer remains a resounding yes.

The first [Bud Light](https://www.budlight.com/) ad during the game (at 18:52) drove a more than 25x increase to their site, and the Bud Light Seltzer Hard Soda ad with [Guy Fieri](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vs0YHD0JoCk) at 21:00 drove a second peak in traffic, with a 15x increase over baseline.

### Financial services: the QR code

We already saw that the Coinbase ad seems to have made social media tick up after its ad aired, but what about traffic to them? The ad drove a 14x increase in traffic. (However, it is worth noting that scanning the QR code in the advertisement took viewers to [drops.coinbase.com](https://drops.coinbase.com/) – this specific hostname is not included in the traffic analyzed for this graph.)

In the classic financial services world, there was another kid on the block that experienced a much bigger bump (140x) in traffic growth. The Greenlight [ad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6JjEdpeca-E) featuring Modern Family’s Phil Dunphy’s (Ty Burrell) purchasing habits aired late in the game, (21:45) but clearly made an impact.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - s1nAiK](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW46SHDEQYPZXRNNQ5XCSQPM.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

### Electric cars (Dr. Evil) takeover

Car commercials have aired for many years during the Super Bowl, teasing new models and technologies. In 2022, electric cars were (again) a popular subject of Super Bowl ads. Bending modern day, 80’s nostalgia, and ancient mythology, BMW rocked down to Electric Avenue as their ad (18:54) resulted in a 14x increase over baseline in traffic.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - m9dRiZ](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW446V4CK2AZ5QF39AFWGPWK.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

However, our data showed that there was a clear winner among automobile makers: the Dr. Evil (one of Mike Myers’s characters from Austin Powers) takeover of General Motors [ad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uEuEBT0TWQE) drove traffic to a peak of over 400x above baseline.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - OJFnho](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW49HEDABCM698VCMNXNKE4A.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Ads from other car vendors including Toyota (5x), Kia (16x), Vroom (70x), Nissan (30x) also generated attention and increased traffic to their websites. Highlighting the importance of charging to the electric car ecosystem, the first ever Super Bowl ad from Wallbox (a manufacturer of electric car chargers) powered a huge increase in traffic to their website, reaching a peak over 2,500x higher than baseline.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - N5mrpd](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW48C23MQMSAKMZDXEEYVYEE.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

### Last but not least

One of the health-related products that had made its mark on the Super Bowl was the early detection medical service Hologic that featured [Mary J. Blige](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Yn8yn6WOP4). They experienced a 140x traffic spike.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - dv9jw1](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW48CQ0J8YK94814TPHKTS04.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Another example that really showed that having a successful Super Bowl commercial doesn't stink was for Irish Spring soap. Their good ‘smelling’ [ad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=656ONxP4t68) drove a traffic increase to their website of nearly 200x over baseline.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - u0Jmnp](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW45ZK3XFY4ZMWHVDVT6FT71.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Among ads for travel-related companies, the biggest increase in traffic we saw was from [Booking.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pp49uUpesb4) (21:23), with the adventures of Idris Elba gaining them a 1.6x bump.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - hawMqi](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW49AR2E28QNEP4J6ADXX4S5.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

Several ads promoted shows and movie trailers, including Dr. Strange 2 and Amazon Prime Video’s _The Rings of Power,_ but the trailer for Jordan Peele’s Nope movie generated a nearly 40x increase in traffic.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - o27bk0](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW46W8AR0F5C6M1V2EKQQP4P.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

### And the winner is…

Popular smart home gadgets appeared to be jealous of the new COVID-19 testing device from Cue Health, but Super Bowl viewers were clearly curious about it. The company's ad drove an astronomical 10,000x increase in traffic to their website after it aired.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns%3D%22http%3A%2F%2Fwww.w3.org%2F2000%2Fsvg%22%20viewBox%3D%220%200%201%201%22%3E%3Crect%20width%3D%221%22%20height%3D%221%22%20fill%3D%22rgb\(243%2C243%2C243\)%22%2F%3E%3C%2Fsvg%3E)![BLOG-988 Embedded Image - cwv6gc](https://blog.cloudflare.com/_image?href=https%3A%2F%2Fblog.cloudflare.com%2F_emdash%2Fapi%2Fmedia%2Ffile%2F01KW496DRPXQWM0854JV46QR56.png&w=715&h=365&f=webp&fit=cover&position=center)

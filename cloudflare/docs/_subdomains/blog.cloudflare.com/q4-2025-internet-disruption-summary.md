# Cable cuts, storms, and DNS: a look at Internet disruptions in Q4 2025

In 2025, we [_observed over 180 Internet disruptions_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/outage-center?dateStart=2025-01-01&dateEnd=2025-12-31) spurred by a variety of causes – some were brief and partial, while others were complete outages lasting for days. In the fourth quarter, we tracked only a single _government-directed_ Internet shutdown, but multiple _cable cuts_ wreaked havoc on connectivity in several countries. _Power outages_ and _extreme weather_ disrupted Internet services in multiple places, and the ongoing _conflict_ in Ukraine impacted connectivity there as well. As always, a number of the disruptions we observed were due to _technical problems_ – with some acknowledged by the relevant providers, while others had unknown causes. In addition, incidents at several hyperscaler _cloud platforms_ and _Cloudflare_ impacted the availability of websites and applications. 

This post is intended as a summary overview of observed and confirmed disruptions and is not an exhaustive or complete list of issues that have occurred during the quarter. These anomalies are detected through significant deviations from expected traffic patterns observed across our network. Check out the [_Cloudflare Radar Outage Center_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/outage-center) for a full list of verified anomalies and confirmed outages. 

## Government-directed

### Tanzania

[ _The Internet was shut down in Tanzania_](https://bsky.app/profile/radar.cloudflare.com/post/3m4df6i7hjk25) on October 29 as [_violent protests_](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/oct/29/tanzania-election-president-samia-suluhu-hassan-poised-to-retain-power) took place during the country’s presidential election. Traffic initially fell around 12:30 local time (09:30 UTC), dropping more than 90% lower than the previous week. The disruption lasted approximately 26 hours, with [_traffic beginning to return_](https://bsky.app/profile/radar.cloudflare.com/post/3m4qec7zdnt2u) around 14:30 local time (11:30 UTC) on October 30. However, that restoration [_proved to be quite brief_](https://bsky.app/profile/radar.cloudflare.com/post/3m4gjngzck72u), with a significant decrease in traffic occurring around 16:15 local time (13:15 UTC), approximately two hours after it returned. This second near-complete outage lasted until November 3, [_when traffic aggressively returned_](https://bsky.app/profile/radar.cloudflare.com/post/3m4g47vasfm2u) after 17:00 local time (14:00 UTC). Nominal drops in [_announced IPv4 and IPv6 address space_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/tz?dateStart=2025-10-29&dateEnd=2025-11-04#announced-ip-address-space) were also observed during the shutdown, but there was never a complete loss of announcements, which would have signified a total disconnection of the country from the Internet. ([_Autonomous systems_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/network-layer/what-is-an-autonomous-system/) announce IP address space to other Internet providers, letting them know what blocks of IP addresses they are responsible for.)

Tanzania’s president later [_expressed sympathy_](https://apnews.com/article/tanzania-samia-suluhu-hassan-internet-shutdown-october-election-1ec66b897e7809865d8971699a7284e0) for the members of the diplomatic community and foreigners residing in the country regarding the impact of the Internet shutdown. Internet and social media services were also [_restricted in 2020_](https://www.dw.com/en/tanzania-internet-slowdown-comes-at-a-high-cost/a-55512732) ahead of the country’s general elections.

## Cable cuts

### Digicel Haiti

Digicel Haiti is unfortunately no stranger to Internet disruptions caused by cable cuts, and the network experienced two more such incidents during the fourth quarter. On October 16, traffic from [_Digicel Haiti (AS27653)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as27653) began to fall at 14:30 local time (18:30 UTC), reaching near zero at 16:00 local time (20:00 UTC). A translated [_X post from the company’s Director General_](https://x.com/jpbrun30/status/1978920959089230003) noted: “ _We advise our clientele that @DigicelHT is experiencing 2 cuts on its international fiber optic infrastructure._ ” Traffic began to recover after 17:00 local time (21:00 UTC), and reached expected levels within the following hour. At 17:33 local time (21:34 UTC), the Director General [_posted_](https://x.com/jpbrun30/status/1978937426841063504) that “ _the first fiber on the international infrastructure has been repaired”_ and service had been restored. 

On November 25, another translated [_X post from the provider’s Director General_](https://x.com/jpbrun30/status/1993283730467963345) stated that its “ _international optical fiber infrastructure on National Road 1_ ” had been cut. We observed traffic dropping on Digicel’s network approximately an hour earlier, with a complete outage observed between 02:00 - 08:00 local time (07:00 - 13:00 UTC). A [_follow-on X post_](https://x.com/jpbrun30/status/1993309357438910484) at 08:22 local time (13:22 UTC) stated that all services had been restored.

### Cybernet/StormFiber (Pakistan)

At 17:30 local time (12:30 UTC) on October 20, Internet traffic for [_Cybernet/StormFiber (AS9541)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as9541) dropped sharply, falling to a level approximately 50% the same time a week prior. At the same time, the network’s announced IPv4 address space dropped by over a third. The cause of these shifts was damage to the [_PEACE_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/submarine-cable/peace-cable) submarine cable, which suffered a cut in the Red Sea near Sudan. 

PEACE is one of several submarine cable systems (including [_IMEWE_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/submarine-cable/imewe) and [_SEA-ME-WE-4_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/submarine-cable/seamewe-4)) that carry international Internet traffic for Pakistani providers. The provider [_pledged to fully restore service_](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/10/24/stormfiber-pledges-full-restoration-by-monday-after-weeklong-internet-disruptions/) by October 27, but traffic and announced IPv4 address space had recovered to near expected levels by around 02:00 local time on October 21 (21:00 UTC on October 20).

### Camtel, MTN Cameroon, Orange Cameroun

Unusual traffic patterns observed across multiple Internet providers in Cameroon on October 23 were reportedly caused by problems on the [_WACS (West Africa Cable System)_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/submarine-cable/west-africa-cable-system-wacs) submarine cable, which connects countries along the west coast of Africa to Portugal. 

A (translated) [_published report_](https://teleasu.tv/internet-graves-perturbations-observees-ce-jeudi-23-octobre-2025/) stated that MTN informed subscribers that “ _following an incident on the WACS fiber optic cable, Internet service is temporarily disrupted_ ” and Orange Cameroun informed subscribers that “ _due to an incident on the international access fiber, Internet service is disrupted._ ” An [_X post from Camtel_](https://x.com/Camtelonline/status/1981424170316464390) stated “ _Cameroon Telecommunications (CAMTEL) wishes to inform the public that a technical incident involving WACS cable equipment in Batoke (LIMBE) occurred in the early hours of 23 October 2025, causing Internet connectivity disruptions throughout the country._ ” 

Traffic across the impacted providers originally fell just at around 05:00 local time (04:00 UTC) before recovering to expected levels around 22:00 local time (21:00 UTC). Traffic across these networks was quite volatile during the day, dropping 90-99% at times. It isn’t clear what caused the visible spikiness in the traffic pattern—possibly attempts to shift Internet traffic to [_other submarine cable systems that connect to Cameroon_](https://www.submarinecablemap.com/country/cameroon). Announced IP address space from [_MTN Cameroon_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as30992?dateStart=2025-10-23&dateEnd=2025-10-23#announced-ip-address-space) and [_Orange Cameroon_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as36912?dateStart=2025-10-23&dateEnd=2025-10-23#announced-ip-address-space) dropped during this period as well, although [_Camtel’s_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as15964?dateStart=2025-10-23&dateEnd=2025-10-23#announced-ip-address-space) announced IP address space did not change.

Connectivity in the [_Central African Republic_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cf) and [_Republic of Congo_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cg) was also reportedly impacted by the WACS issues.

### Claro Dominicana

On December 9, we saw traffic from [_Claro Dominicana (AS6400)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as6400), an Internet provider in the Dominican Republic, drop sharply around 12:15 local time (16:15 UTC). Traffic levels fell again around 14:15 local time (18:15 UTC), bottoming out 77% lower than the previous week before quickly returning to expected levels. The connectivity disruption was likely caused by two fiber optic outages, as an [_X post from the provider_](https://x.com/ClaroRD/status/1998468046311002183) during the outage noted that they were “causing intermittency and slowness in some services.” A [_subsequent post on X_](https://x.com/ClaroRD/status/1998496113838764343) from Claro stated that technicians had restored Internet services nationwide by repairing the severed fiber optic cables.

## Power outages

### Dominican Republic

According to a (translated) [_X post from the Empresa de Transmisión Eléctrica Dominicana_](https://x.com/ETED_RD/status/1988326178219061450) (ETED), a transmission line outage caused an interruption in electrical service in the [_Dominican Republic_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/do) on November 11. This power outage impacted Internet traffic from the country, resulting in a [_nearly 50% drop in traffic_](https://noc.social/@cloudflareradar/115533081511310085) compared to the prior week, starting at 13:15 local time (17:15 UTC). Traffic levels remained lower until approximately 02:00 local time (06:00 UTC) on December 12, with a later [_(translated) X post from ETED_](https://x.com/ETED_RD/status/1988575130990330153) noting “ _At 2:20 a.m. we have completed the recovery of the national electrical system, supplying 96% of the demand…_ ”

A subsequent [_technical report found_](https://dominicantoday.com/dr/local/2025/11/27/manual-line-disconnection-triggered-nationwide-blackout-report-says/) that “ _the blackout began at the 138 kV San Pedro de Macorís I substation, where a live line was manually disconnected, triggering a high-intensity short circuit. Protection systems responded immediately, but the fault caused several nearby lines to disconnect, separating 575 MW of generation in the eastern region from the rest of the grid. The imbalance caused major power plants to trip automatically as part of their built-in safety mechanisms._ ”

### Kenya

On December 9, a [_major power outage_](https://www.tuko.co.ke/kenya/612181-kenya-power-reveals-7-pm-nationwide-blackout-multiple-regions/) impacted multiple regions across [_Kenya_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ke). Kenya Power explained that the outage “ _was triggered by an incident on the regional Kenya-Uganda interconnected power network, which caused a disturbance on the Kenyan side of the system_ ” and claimed that “ _[p]ower was restored to most of the affected areas within approximately 30 minutes._ ” However, impacts to Internet connectivity lasted for nearly four hours, between 19:15 - 23:00 local time (16:15 - 20:00 UTC). The power outage caused traffic to drop as much as 18% at a national level, with the traffic shifts most visible in [_Nakuru County_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/7668902) and [_Kaimbu County_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/192709).

## Military action

### Odesa, Ukraine

[ _Russian drone strikes_](https://odessa-journal.com/russia-carried-out-a-massive-drone-attack-on-the-odessa-region) on the [_Odesa region_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/698738) in [_Ukraine_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/ua) on December 12 damaged warehouses and energy infrastructure, with the latter causing power outages in parts of the region. Those outages disrupted Internet connectivity, resulting in [_traffic dropping by as much as 57%_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/2000993223406211327?s=20) as compared to the prior week. After the initial drop at midnight on December 13 (22:00 UTC on December 12), traffic gradually recovered over the following several days, returning to expected levels around 14:30 local time (12:30 UTC) on December 16.

## Weather

### Jamaica

[ _Hurricane Melissa_](https://www.nytimes.com/live/2025/10/28/weather/hurricane-melissa-jamaica-landfall?smid=url-share#df989e67-a90e-50fb-92d0-8d5d52f76e84) made landfall on [_Jamaica_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/jm) on October 28 and left a trail of damage and destruction in its path. Associated [_power outages_](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2025/10/28/eyeonmelissa-35-jps-customers-without-power/) and infrastructure damage impacted Internet connectivity, causing traffic to initially [_drop by approximately half_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1983266694715084866), [_starting_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1983217966347866383) around 06:15 local time (11:15 UTC), ultimately reaching as much as [_70% lower_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1983357587707048103) than the previous week. Internet traffic from Jamaica remained well below pre-hurricane levels for several days, and ultimately started to make greater progress towards expected levels [_during the morning of November 4_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1985708253872107713?s=20). It can often take weeks or months for Internet traffic from a country to return to “normal” levels following storms that cause massive and widespread damage – while power may be largely restored within several days, damage to physical infrastructure takes significantly longer to address.

### Sri Lanka & Indonesia

On November 26, [_Cyclone Senyar_](https://apnews.com/article/indonesia-sri-lanka-thailand-malaysia-floods-landsides-aa9947df1f6192a3c6c72ef58659d4d2) caused catastrophic floods and landslides in [_Sri Lanka_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/lk) and [_Indonesia_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/id), killing over 1,000 people and damaging telecommunications and power infrastructure across these countries. The infrastructure damage resulted in [_disruptions to Internet connectivity_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1996233525989720083), and resultant lower traffic levels, across multiple regions.

In Sri Lanka, regions outside the main Western Province were the most affected, and several provinces saw traffic drop [_between 80% and 95%_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1996233528032301513) as compared to the prior week, including [_North Western_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1232860?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14), [_Southern_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1227618?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14), [_Uva_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1225265?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14), [_Eastern_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/8133521?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14), [_Northern_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/7671049?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14), [_North Central_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1232870?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14), and [_Sabaragamuwa_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1228435?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14).

In [_Indonesia_](https://x.com/CloudflareRadar/status/1996233530267885938), [_Aceh_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1215638?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14) and the Sumatra regions saw the biggest Internet disruptions. In Aceh, traffic initially dropped over 75% as compared to the previous week. In Sumatra, [_North Sumatra_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/traffic/1213642?dateStart=2025-11-24&dateEnd=2025-12-14) was the most affected, with an early 30% drop as compared to the previous week, before starting to recover more actively the following week.

## Known or unspecified technical problems

### Smartfren (Indonesia)

On October 3, subscribers to Indonesian Internet provider [_Smartfren (AS18004_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as18004)) experienced a service disruption. The issues were [_acknowledged by the provider in an X post_](https://x.com/smartfrenworld/status/1973957300466643203), which stated (in translation), “ _Currently, telephone, SMS and data services are experiencing problems in several areas._ ” Traffic from the provider fell as much as 84%, starting around 09:00 local time (02:00 UTC). The disruption lasted for approximately eight hours, as traffic returned to expected levels around 17:00 local time (10:00 UTC). Smartfren did not provide any additional information on what caused the service problems.

### Vodafone UK

Major British Internet provider Vodafone UK ([_AS5378_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as5378) & [_AS25135_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as25135)) experienced a brief service outage on October 23. At 15:00 local time (14:00 UTC), traffic on both Vodafone [_ASNs_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/network-layer/what-is-an-autonomous-system/) dropped to zero. Announced IPv4 address space from [_AS5378_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as5378?dateStart=2025-10-13&dateEnd=2025-10-13#announced-ip-address-space) fell by 75%, while announced IPv4 address space from [_AS25135_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/routing/as25135?dateStart=2025-10-13&dateEnd=2025-10-13#announced-ip-address-space) disappeared entirely. Both Internet traffic and address space recovered two hours later, returning to expected levels around 17:00 local time (16:00 UTC). Vodafone did not provide any information on their social media channels about the cause of the outage, and their [_network status checker page_](https://www.vodafone.co.uk/network/status-checker) was also unavailable during the outage.

### Fastweb (Italy)

According to a [_published report_](https://tg24.sky.it/tecnologia/2025/10/22/fastweb-down-problemi-internet-oggi), a DNS resolution issue disrupted Internet services for customers of Italian provider [_Fastweb (AS12874)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as12874) on October 22, causing observed traffic volumes to drop by over 75%. Fastweb [_acknowledged the issue_](https://www.firstonline.info/en/fastweb-down-oggi-internet-bloccato-in-tutta-italia-migliaia-di-segnalazioni/), which impacted wired Internet customers between 09:30 - 13:00 local time (08:30 - 12:00 UTC).

Although not an Internet outage caused by connectivity failure, the impact of DNS resolution issues on Internet traffic is very similar. When a provider’s [_DNS resolver_](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/dns/dns-server-types/) is experiencing problems, switching to a service like Cloudflare’s [_1.1.1.1 public DNS resolver_](https://1.1.1.1/dns) will often restore connectivity.

### SBIN, MTN Benin, Etisalat Benin

On December 7, a concurrent drop in traffic was observed across [_SBIN (AS28683)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as28683), [_MTN Benin (AS37424)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as37424), and [_Etisalat Benin (AS37136)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as37136). Between 18:30 - 19:30 local time (17:30 - 18:30 UTC), traffic dropped as much as 80% as compared to the prior week at a country level, nearly 100% at Etisalat and MTN, and over 80% at SBIN.

While an [_attempted coup_](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/soldiers-benins-national-television-claim-have-seized-power-2025-12-07/) had taken place earlier in the day, it is unclear whether the observed Internet disruption was related in any way. From a routing perspective, all three impacted networks share [_Cogent (AS174)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as174) as an upstream provider, so a localized issue at Cogent may have contributed to the brief outage. 

### Cellcom Israel

According to a [_reported announcement_](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/2gpt1kt35) from Israeli provider [_Cellcom (AS1680)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as1680), on December 18, there was “ _a malfunction affecting Internet connectivity that is impacting some of our customers._ ” This malfunction dropped traffic nearly 70% as compared to the prior week, and occurred between 09:30 - 11:00 local time (07:30 - 09:00 UTC). The “malfunction” may have been a DNS failure, according to a [_published report_](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/419552).

### Partner Communications (Israel)

Closing out 2025, on December 30, a major technical failure at Israeli provider [_Partner Communications (AS12400)_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/as12400) [_disrupted_](https://www.ynetnews.com/tech-and-digital/article/hjewkibnwe) mobile, TV, and Internet services across the country. Internet traffic from Partner fell by two-thirds as compared to the previous week between 14:00 - 15:00 local time (12:00 - 13:00 UTC). During the outage, queries to Cloudflare’s 1.1.1.1 public DNS resolver spiked, suggesting that the problem may have been related to Partner’s DNS infrastructure. However, the provider did not publicly confirm what caused the outage.

## Cloud Platforms

During the fourth quarter, we launched a new [_Cloud Observatory_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory) page on Radar that tracks availability and performance issues at a region level across hyperscaler cloud platforms, including [_Amazon Web Services_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/amazon), [_Microsoft Azure_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/microsoft), [_Google Cloud Platform_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/google), and [_Oracle Cloud Infrastructure_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/oracle).

### Amazon Web Services

On October 20, the Amazon Web Services us-east-1 region in Northern Virginia experienced “[ _increased error rates and latencies_](https://health.aws.amazon.com/health/status?eventID=arn:aws:health:us-east-1::event/MULTIPLE_SERVICES/AWS_MULTIPLE_SERVICES_OPERATIONAL_ISSUE/AWS_MULTIPLE_SERVICES_OPERATIONAL_ISSUE_BA540_514A652BE1A)” that affected multiple services within the region. The issues impacted not only customers with public-facing Web sites and applications that rely on infrastructure within the region, but also Cloudflare customers that have origin resources hosted in us-east-1.

We began to see the impact of the problems around 06:30 UTC, as the share of [_error_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/amazon/us-east-1?dateStart=2025-10-20&dateEnd=2025-10-21#success-rate) ([_5xx-class_](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Status#server_error_responses)) responses began to climb, reaching as high as 17% around 08:00 UTC. The number of [_failures encountered when attempting to connect to origins_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/amazon/us-east-1?dateStart=2025-10-20&dateEnd=2025-10-21#connection-failures) in us-east-1 climbed as well, peaking around 12:00 UTC.

The impact could also be clearly seen in key network performance metrics, which remained elevated throughout the incident, returning to normal levels just before the end of the incident, around 23:00 UTC. Both [_TCP_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/amazon/us-east-1?dateStart=2025-10-20&dateEnd=2025-10-21#tcp-handshake-duration) and [_TLS_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/amazon/us-east-1?dateStart=2025-10-20&dateEnd=2025-10-21#tls-handshake-duration) handshake durations got progressively worse throughout the incident—these metrics measure the amount of time needed for Cloudflare to establish TCP and TLS connections respectively with customer origin servers in us-east-1. In addition, the amount of time elapsed before Cloudflare [_received response headers_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/amazon/us-east-1/#response-header-receive-duration) from the origin increased significantly during the first several hours of the incident, before gradually returning to expected levels. 

### Microsoft Azure

On October 29, Microsoft Azure experienced an [_incident_](https://azure.status.microsoft/en-us/status/history/?trackingId=YKYN-BWZ) impacting [_Azure Front Door_](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/products/frontdoor), its content delivery network service. According to [_Azure's report on the incident_](https://azure.status.microsoft/en-us/status/history/?trackingId=YKYN-BWZ), “ _A specific sequence of customer configuration changes, performed across two different control plane build versions, resulted in incompatible customer configuration metadata being generated. These customer configuration changes themselves were valid and non-malicious – however they produced metadata that, when deployed to edge site servers, exposed a latent bug in the data plane. This incompatibility triggered a crash during asynchronous processing within the data plane service._ ”

The incident report marked the start time at 15:41 UTC, although we observed the volume of [_failed connection attempts_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/microsoft/global?dateStart=2025-10-29&dateEnd=2025-10-30#connection-failures) to Azure-hosted origins begin to climb about 45 minutes prior. The TCP and TLS handshake metrics also became more volatile during the incident period, with [_TCP handshakes_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/microsoft/global?dateStart=2025-10-29&dateEnd=2025-10-30#tcp-handshake-duration) taking over 50% longer at times, and [_TLS handshakes_](https://radar.cloudflare.com/cloud-observatory/microsoft/global?dateStart=2025-10-29&dateEnd=2025-10-30#tls-handshake-duration) taking nearly 200% longer at peak. The impacted metrics began to improve after 20:00 UTC, and according to Microsoft, the incident ended at 00:05 UTC on October 30.

## Cloudflare

In addition to the outages discussed above, Cloudflare also experienced two disruptions during the fourth quarter. While these were not Internet outages in the classic sense, they did prevent users from accessing Web sites and applications delivered and protected by Cloudflare when they occurred.

The first incident took place on November 18, and was caused by a software failure triggered by a change to one of our database systems' permissions, which caused the database to output multiple entries into a “feature file” used by our Bot Management system. Additional details, including a root cause analysis and timeline, can be found in the associated [_blog post_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage/).

The second incident occurred on December 5, and impacted a subset of customers, accounting for approximately 28% of all HTTP traffic served by Cloudflare. It was triggered by changes being made to our request body parsing logic while attempting to detect and mitigate a newly disclosed industry-wide React Server Components vulnerability. A post-mortem [_blog post_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/5-december-2025-outage/) contains additional details, including a root cause analysis and timeline.

For more information about the work underway at Cloudflare to prevent outages like these from happening again, check out our [_blog post_](https://blog.cloudflare.com/fail-small-resilience-plan/) detailing “Code Orange: Fail Small.”

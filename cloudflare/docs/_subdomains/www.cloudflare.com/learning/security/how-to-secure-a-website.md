## What is website security?

Website security is the practice, methods, and protocol aimed at protecting and securing a website and its data. The methods are designed to [prevent a wide range of cyber security threats](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/zero-trust/threat-defense/), such as [DDoS attacks](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ddos/what-is-a-ddos-attack/), [SQL injection attacks](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/security/threats/sql-injection/) or [malware](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ddos/glossary/malware/).

Cyber security threats can have wide and devastating impact for businesses, such as economic costs, such as loss in revenue and expenses for remediation, damaged brand reputation from poor media coverage and loss of trust, and regulatory costs such as significant fines for failing to protect PII data in accordance with GDPR or HIPAA.

## Why is website security important?

Implementing website security measures are critical to [protecting businesses from cyber attacks](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/zero-trust/threat-defense/). Blocking potential threats help organizations:

  * **Protect sensitive data.** Websites often contain or host sensitive [personal information](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/privacy/what-is-personal-information/), such as names, addresses, phone numbers, or financial details (like credit card information). Compromised data can lead to a significant loss of customer trust, legal issues, and potential financial losses.

  * **Avoid business disruption.** Attackers can cause website outages or slow down performance through targeted [bot attacks](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/bots/what-is-a-bot-attack/) or DDoS attacks, resulting in significant business disruption. This can be costly for businesses in [ecommerce](https://www.cloudflare.com/ecommerce/).

  * **Comply with regulatory requirements.** Most businesses are accountable to a variety of federal and international regulations, including HIPAA, [Payment Card Industry](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/privacy/what-is-pci-dss-compliance/), and GDPR. Non-compliance will result in heavy fines and penalties.

  * **Improve SEO and website visibility.** Search engines such as Google or Bing penalize businesses for infected or unsecured websites. By taking steps to secure their websites, businesses can boost their SEO and ensure their websites will be indexed properly.




## How to secure a website?

There are many ways to secure a website. Many of the best practices fall within four categories: technical measures, coding and design, access control and user management, and backup and recovery plans.

  * Technical measures include implementing solutions and tools to protect a website, such as [Secure Sockets Layer (SSL)](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ssl/what-is-ssl/) certificates for encryption, [web application firewalls (WAF)](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ddos/glossary/web-application-firewall-waf/) to filter and block malicious traffic, security plugins or software to scan for threats, [content delivery networks (CDN)](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/cdn/what-is-a-cdn/) to block DDoS attacks, and enacting regular updates and patches to the CMS, plugins, and website themes.

  * Coding and design best practices can minimize security risks. Examples include input validating to protect against SQL injection attacks, user password safe storage, secure user authentication and session management, and minimizing the use of third-party code.

  * [Access control](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/access-management/what-is-access-control/) and user management is the practice of managing who has access to the backend of the website. Examples of access control include using strong passwords and changing them regularly, implementing [two-factor authentication](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/access-management/what-is-two-factor-authentication/), limiting administrative access, regularly reviewing user access, and removing unnecessary user permissions.

  * Backup and recovery plans help businesses prepare for the worse-case scenarios. Best practices look like regular data backups, testing backup recovery procedures, and making sure backup data are secure.

## FAQs

#### What is website security?

Website security refers to the practices, methods, and protocols used to protect a website and its data from a wide range of cyber threats, such as DDoS attacks, SQL injection, or malware.

#### Why is it important to secure a website?

Securing a website is critical for protecting sensitive customer data, avoiding business disruptions from outages, complying with data protection regulations like GDPR, and improving the site's SEO by avoiding penalties from search engines.

#### What are some technical measures for securing a website?

Technical measures include using SSL certificates to encrypt data and authenticate the web host for clients, deploying a web application firewall (WAF) to block malicious traffic, using security plugins to scan for threats, and regularly updating your CMS, plugins, and themes.

#### How can coding and design practices improve website security?

Secure coding practices, such as validating user inputs to protect against SQL injection and securely managing user authentication and sessions, can minimize security risks from the ground up. Minimizing the use of third-party code also reduces potential vulnerabilities.

#### What is the role of access control in website security?

Access control involves managing who has access to the backend of your website. Best practices include using strong passwords, implementing two-factor authentication, limiting administrative access to only those who need it, and regularly reviewing and removing unnecessary user permissions.

#### Why is having a backup and recovery plan important?

A backup and recovery plan helps a business prepare for worst-case scenarios. This involves performing regular data backups, testing the recovery procedures, and ensuring that the backup data itself is stored securely.
